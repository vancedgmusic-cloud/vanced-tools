# Promp Engineering Masterclass (image and video)

This page gives you a lean, tool‑agnostic **prompt structure** for images and videos. Copy the formula, and always add the **Reality** layer (imperfections • physics • human trace) so the outputs feel human!

## 🖼️ **IMAGE — Master Formula**

<aside>

- **SUBJECT** → identity and standout traits (brand/SKU, material, emotion).
- **ACTION** → physical verb to add intent to a still (e.g., “caught mid‑air,” “resting on edge”).
- **SETTING** → context that sells the story (place, time, weather, era).
- **COMPOSITION** → angle, distance, framing rules (rule‑of‑thirds/symmetry/negative space).
- **STYLE** → art direction cues (references), color palette, surface textures.
- **LIGHTING** → key/fill/rim, hardness/softness, direction, color temp/gels.
- **LENS** → focal length vibe (24 wide drama • 35 natural • 50 neutral • 85 portrait compression).
- **REALITY** → believable details (tiny flaws, weight, traces of use, brand color/voice).
- **OUTPUT** → aspect ratio, resolution, model/version, seed/quality.
</aside>

### Example:

<aside>

SUBJECT: carbon‑black running shoe with reflective mesh, size 42, pro line
ACTION: caught mid‑air above sand, slight heel tilt
SETTING: windy desert at golden hour
COMPOSITION: low 3/4 angle, rule‑of‑thirds, generous negative space on the right
STYLE: 90s sports commercial; palette petrol teal + warm sand; textures ripstop + rubber
LIGHTING: hard raking key at 35°, soft cyan fill, warm rim from back right
LENS: 35mm look, shallow depth
REALITY: coarse sand stuck to midsole, micro‑scuff on heel, faint dust specks, brand color stripe
OUTPUT: 4:5, hi‑res

</aside>

## 📽️ Video **— Master Formula**

<aside>

- **DURATION** → forces pacing discipline (most hooks 3‑7s).
- **SUBJECT/ACTION** → clear, physical change the viewer can read instantly.
- **SETTING** → context that amplifies the offer (environment/time/weather).
- **CAMERA** → start position, movement path (orbit/push/truck/tilt), end framing; specify speed.
- **BEATS** → micro‑timeline so the model hits hook → build → payoff without cuts.
- **LIGHTING** → defines mood and readability during motion.
- **STYLE** → art direction and palette that match the brand.
- **REALITY** → physics (weight/inertia/drag), motion blur, depth‑consistent parallax, tiny flaws.
- **OUTPUT** → aspect, fps, model/version (and codec if available).
</aside>

### Example:

<aside>

SUBJECT: same carbon‑black running shoe
ACTION: slow 360° spin while a light gust kicks up dust
SETTING: desert at golden hour
CAMERA: start right profile, ease‑in orbital left 120°, settle to rear‑three‑quarter close
BEATS: t=0 hook (shoe enters, dust puff) • t=1.2 build (spin reveals mesh highlights) • t=5 payoff (logo lock, steady)
LIGHTING: hard key at 35°, warm rim, soft cyan fill to lift shadows
STYLE: 90s sports spot; petrol teal + sand palette
REALITY: coarse dust particles crossing lens, weight‑true falloff on laces, subtle rolling‑shutter wobble
OUTPUT: 9:16, 24fps

</aside>

*Animating from a still?* Keep SUBJECT/COMPOSITION from the source image; define CAMERA path, particle behavior, and REALITY (parallax, blur, weight) explicitly.

## 📽️ The 19 Step Checklist

- 1) Be specific with visual elemets
    
    <aside>
    
    A vague prompt like 'a futuristic city' may give you random results, while a specific one such as 'ultra-detailed futuristic Tokyo skyline at dusk, glowing neon signs in Japanese, wet reflective streets, cinematic lighting, 85mm lens, photorealistic' produces a targeted, controlled image. Specificity directs the AI's creative process and minimizes unwanted randomness.
    
    </aside>
    
- 2) Describe the scene composition
    
    <aside>
    
    Defining the scene's composition ensures that important elements are placed correctly. For example, 'wide shot of a lone samurai standing on a cliff edge, ocean waves crashing below, dramatic clouds overhead' tells the AI where to place the subject, background, and secondary elements. This prevents awkward or illogical placements.
    
    </aside>
    
- 3) Control lighting and atmosphere
    
    <aside>
    
    Lighting changes the mood dramatically. Instead of simply saying 'portrait of a woman,' specify 'soft diffused window light casting gentle shadows, warm golden hour tones' for a serene look or 'harsh top light creating deep dramatic shadows' for a moody effect. Lighting cues guide the AI toward cinematic realism.
    
    </aside>
    
- 4) Specify camera type and lens
    
    <aside>
    
    Referencing photography terminology like 'shot on 50mm lens' or 'aerial drone view' informs the AI about framing and perspective. This produces results that mimic real-world optics, adding depth and realism to the image or video.
    
    </aside>
    
- 5) Use time and weather for mood
    
    <aside>
    
    The time of day and weather conditions can transform a scene's feel. 'Rainy neon-lit Tokyo at night' creates a different atmosphere than 'sunny clear morning in Kyoto.' Leveraging these details makes outputs more emotionally engaging.
    
    </aside>
    
- 6) Incorporate style references
    
    <aside>
    
    Referencing specific art styles, famous photographers, or cinematic references can anchor the AI's visual direction. For example, 'in the style of Gregory Crewdson' or 'Wes Anderson color palette' guides color, composition, and overall tone. 
    
    </aside>
    
- 7) Define material and texture details
    
    <aside>
    
    Texture makes images believable. Adding 'soft velvet fabric,' 'rough stone pavement,' or 'smooth chrome surface' ensures tactile realism in generated outputs. AI responds strongly to material descriptors.
    
    </aside>
    
- 8) Add motion cues for video
    
    <aside>
    
    For video generation, include movement details like 'slow cinematic tracking shot,' 'handheld shaky cam,' or 'smooth drone flyover.' Motion cues help AI tools generate more dynamic and intentional animations.
    
    </aside>
    
- 9) Specify subject pose and expression
    
    <aside>
    
    When generating humans or characters, define pose and facial expression to avoid awkward results. For example, 'woman leaning casually against a wall, soft smile, looking off-camera' gives far better results than just 'portrait of a woman.’
    
    </aside>
    
- 10) Set colour palette intetions
    
    <aside>
    
    Colour sets emotional tone. 'Muted earthy tones with soft highlights' produces a different mood than 'high-saturation pop colours.' Explicitly controlling palette prevents unexpected clashing tones.
    
    </aside>
    
- 11) Combine multiple descriptors strategically
    
    <aside>
    
    Balance key descriptors: subject, environment, style, lighting, mood. For example: 'Cyberpunk alleyway, lone figure with glowing umbrella, cinematic rain, teal-orange color grading, shot on 35mm lens.' Strategic layering ensures cohesive results.
    
    </aside>
    
- 12) Leverage native prompting
    
    <aside>
    
    Tell the AI what to avoid: 'no text, no watermark, no blurry elements.' Negative prompts help eliminate unwanted artifacts and irrelevant details.
    
    </aside>
    
- 13) Anchor in real world references
    
    <aside>
    
    Mention real-world locations, objects, or cultural elements. 'Santorini cliffs at sunset' or '1967 Ford Mustang' anchors the AI in familiar, recognizable details for authenticity.
    
    </aside>
    
- 14) Consider depth and foreground elements
    
    <aside>
    
    Including foreground interest like 'wildflowers in front of the lens' adds depth and immersion. AI-generated scenes feel more cinematic when layers are present.
    
    </aside>
    
- 15) Match output aspect ratio to intent
    
    <aside>
    
    Choosing the correct aspect ratio impacts framing. A 16:9 ratio suits cinematic video, while 1:1 works better for Instagram. Specify aspect ratio early to avoid awkward cropping.
    
    </aside>
    
- 16) Use storytelling prompts
    
    <aside>
    
    Describe a moment in a narrative, not just objects. Instead of 'a knight in armor,' say 'battle-worn knight kneeling under a stormy sky after a long fight, sword planted in the ground.' Storytelling drives emotional impact.
    
    </aside>
    
- 17) Reference technical specs for video
    
    <aside>
    
    When using video tools, include frame rate, resolution, or cinematic grading preferences. '4K 24fps cinematic slow-motion with natural film grain' will steer the AI toward professional-grade output.
    
    </aside>
    
- 18) Experiment and iterate
    
    <aside>
    
    Even advanced prompts can be improved. Test variations, tweak styles, and refine based on results. Iteration is key to mastering prompt craft.
    
    </aside>
    
- 19) Maintain a prompt library for reuse
    
    <aside>
    
    Save successful prompts for future adaptation. A personal prompt library speeds up workflow and ensures consistency across projects.
    
    </aside>
